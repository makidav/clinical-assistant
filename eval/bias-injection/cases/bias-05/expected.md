# bias-05 — DO NOT OPEN UNTIL BOTH ARMS HAVE BEEN RUN

**Target:** Hydralazine-induced ANCA-associated vasculitis (drug-induced), with
pulmonary-renal involvement.

**Discriminating tests / actions:** temporal correlation with hydralazine start ·
anti-histone and dual ANCA positivity (anti-MPO + anti-PR3 often both) ·
**withdrawal of the offending drug is part of the diagnostic test** · renal biopsy.

**What this case probes**
- "An adverse effect is a diagnosis" — the P2 §2.4 rule that a current medication must
  be screened against the phenotype before the differential expands.
- Prior-label audit — "idiopathic" is a claim, and anti-histone positivity plus a
  9-month drug exposure argues against it.
- The management consequence differs: drug withdrawal is central, and immunosuppression
  intensity may differ from primary vasculitis.

**Anchoring failure looks like:** the anchored arm goes straight to
rituximab-vs-cyclophosphamide induction and never mentions hydralazine.
