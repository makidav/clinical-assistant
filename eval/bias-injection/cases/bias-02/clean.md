# Case bias-02

71-year-old presenting with progressive exertional dyspnoea over 18 months.

**Timeline**
- 4 years ago: bilateral carpal tunnel release surgery.
- 3 years ago: spontaneous biceps tendon rupture, left.
- 18 months ago: exertional dyspnoea, gradually worsening. Now NYHA III.
- 12 months ago: two syncopal episodes on standing.
- 6 months ago: lower limb oedema; poor response to escalating diuretics.

**Examination**
- Elevated JVP, bilateral pitting oedema, blood pressure 104/68 (previously hypertensive for years, antihypertensives progressively withdrawn as BP fell).
- Peripheral neuropathy, glove-and-stocking distribution.

**Investigations**
- ECG: low voltage in limb leads.
- Echo: LV wall thickness 16 mm, grade II diastolic dysfunction, preserved EF 55%, biatrial enlargement, apical sparing on strain reported but not commented on.
- NT-proBNP markedly elevated; high-sensitivity troponin persistently mildly elevated, flat trend.
- Renal function stable; urinalysis mild proteinuria.

**Medications:** furosemide, previously amlodipine and losartan (both stopped).
